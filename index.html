Skill Moves Training Ladder — Vol. 1, Session Plan #2

20–30 yd grid. 5–6 mannequins laddered down, 3 strides apart. Players work through the gaps performing moves left and right at each mannequin.

Moves: Step Over · Drag Circle · Cut Circle · L-Turn · V-Move · Matthews · Scissors · Maradona

Progression → Remove last mannequin. Coach stands at end, steps left or right. Player reacts with skill move and escapes through gate in the opposite direction.
⚔️ Station 2 — Coach P · Super 6 #3: Reversed Mini Goals · 20 min
20×20 grid. Two mini goals side by side in the center, facing opposite end lines. Two lines on opposite ends. Coach plays ball in to the attacker — 1v1 against the defender.

One goal faces the attacker (immediate). The other faces away — requires getting around and behind it. That's where the skill move lives.

Cues: reading which goal is available before receiving · move commits defender, not just performed · sharp acceleration out of the move
🌐 SSG — Reversed End Zone Goals · 20 min
Two mini goals side by side on each end. Players locked into end zones.

Support players can enter the end zone after the ball arrives. Defenders may recover in — but cannot be in the zone before the ball arrives.

Source: Coach's collection. Carries the reversed goal concept from Station 2 into a team context.

🎯 Phase 1 — Ball Mastery · Coach CK · ~12–15 min
Kevin Middleton / 360TFT — V-Cone Setup

Inside Taps (Rhythm and Balance)
Perform quick, continuous touches using the inside of both feet while moving towards the big cone at the bottom of the V. Once there, head to the other side of the V.

Inside/Outside (Manipulating the Ball)
Dribble forward using alternating inside and outside touches to move smoothly from the top of the V to the bottom, and back again.

Cruyff Turn (Turning Out of Pressure)
Approach the bottom big cone at pace and, just before reaching it, plant your standing foot next to the ball. Use the inside of your other foot to drag the ball behind your standing leg and turn back toward the previous direction.

General Tip (Middleton): The cones are defenders — when turning, keep the ball away from the defenders, don't turn into them.
📋 Phase 2 — Teach + Color Game · Coach Rozier · ~35–40 min
Step 1 — Board: Draw all four basic situations. Label exactly as Peters defines them.
Step 2 — Grass: Walk each situation. No ball, no defender, no speed. Then straight into the Color Game.

FRONTAL — Teammate Facing Opponent
Both attackers on same line, facing the defender. Move toward him — create the dilemma. Cue: "Who does the defender commit to?"

LATERAL — Teammate Beside The Opponent
Support player beside the defender. Draw him first, then release. Cue: "Is he tracking the ball or the support?"

BEHIND — Teammate Behind The Opponent
Support in the passing lane behind the defender. Gaze feint before you play through. Cue: "Through or around?"

BEFORE — Teammate In Front Of Opponent
Teammate facing own goal, defender behind them. Pass must be precise. Follow immediately. Cue: "Are you ready to follow? Do you know what you're doing before the ball arrives?"

COLOR GAME — Peters & Schumacher p. 72–73
22×28m corridor. Coach passes + calls RED/BLUE/ORANGE. Called color = cone pair the support player attacks toward. First 5–7 min passive — no defender. Add live defender after. Counter-goals on sidelines for defender wins.
🌐 Phase 3 — SSG · 3v3 + 1 Neutral · ~25–30 min
Peters & Schumacher p. 232–233

2 passers outside (8–10m left + right of box) · 3v3 inside · 1 neutral · King in goal.

Numbers → Space:
3v3 + 1 neutral → 20 yd width (6-yd box)
3v3 + 2 neutrals → 26 yd width
4v4 + 2 neutrals → 32 yd width · same depth scaling

Process: Passers make 1–2 lateral passes then play a vertical ball to a free runner — NOT to the neutral. Attacker finishes or plays 4v3 overload. Defenders win ball → return to passer outside → roles switch.

Condition (first 15 min): Must use the neutral before finishing. Goal without neutral doesn't count.
After 15 min: Drop the condition — free game. Does the habit carry?

Scoring: attack scores → stays on. Attack misses/GK saves → switch. Offside applies in 5m zone.

🎯 Phase 1 — Ball Mastery (Blended) · 20 min
Segment A — V-Cone Retention (8 min)

V-cone formation. Players with a ball each. Chain Inside Taps into Inside/Outside back to back at speed through the V. Close with Player's Choice — best owned move, game speed.

Cues: touches light and quick · eyes up after every touch · commit to the combo, no hesitation between moves

Segment B — Partner Touch: 4v0 Passing Square (12 min)

10×10 yd grid. Four players (A–D) anchored in corners. Ball travels corner to corner — 2 touches, inside-right-foot only (~1.5 min) → inside-left-foot only (~1.5 min) → 45-sec scoring competition right foot (completed pass = +1, ball out or broken restriction = −1).

Cues: open the body · receive across the standing leg · weight the pass so it arrives playable · check target before playing

Source: Dan Blank, Soccer iQ Presents... Possession, pp. 78–79
📋 Teach — Reading the Pass Before It Arrives · 10 min
No defender. No speed. Two ideas, back to back.

"What's Next?" — A player can't play fast if he waits until after the ball arrives to make decisions. He needs to know his options before it gets there. Ask it constantly, even off the ball.

Receiving with the Proper Foot — The ball must arrive on whichever foot is farthest from pressure. That detail determines whether he can escape, advance, and set up the next pass. Don't assume two touches — often one is all there is.

Walk it. Freeze it. Ask: "What's your next pass — before it gets to you?"

Source: Dan Blank, Soccer iQ Presents... Possession, pp. 17–19, 25–26
↻ Phase 2 — 3v0 → 3v1 Progression · 20 min
3v0 (~8 min) — Same 10×10 grid, one corner empty. Ball travels into the empty corner. Passer must delay until the runner is roughly halfway across — or the ball arrives before anyone's there.

3v1 (~12 min) — Introduce a defender. Whoever gives it away swaps in. Ball can now change direction on the fly — supporting players must read pressure and decide where support is needed.

Cues (Passing Angles and Empathy): support at the two corners forming a right angle with the ball — never the diagonal. Don't force a bad angle — leave it alone rather than gamble.

Source: Dan Blank, Soccer iQ Presents... Possession, pp. 22–24, 80–84
🌐 Phase 3 — SSG: Possession Square · 35 min
Stage 1 — Targets (18 min)
2v2 inside the grid. Target players beyond each end line — can't enter, return ball to whoever played it in. Scoring: 5 consecutive passes = 1 pt · play ball to target = 1 pt · do both in same sequence = 2 pts. First 12 min conditioned (outside players 2-touch), drop condition for final 6 min.

Stage 2 — Pop-Up Goals (17 min)
Pull targets. Set a pop-up goal each end line. Same grid or slightly expanded. Scoring: 5 passes then score = 3 pts · score without 5 passes = 1 pt. First 10 min conditioned (1–2 touch to score), final 7 min free.

Cues both stages: "What's Next?" and proper-foot receiving under real pressure. The goals raise the consequence — the principles don't change.

Scaling: 6 = 2v2 + 2 outside · 8–10 = 2v2 + 4 outside or 3v3 + 2 outside · 10 = 3v3 + 4 outside · 12+ = 4v4 (target-men variant: 3-touch, score end to end)

Lunge Switches into Sprint
3 quick jump lunges → 10m sprint. Activates hip flexors and glutes. No pause between last lunge and first step.
A-Skip into Sprint
A-Skip for 5–8m → full sprint. Carry the knee drive from the skip into the sprint — don't reset posture between the two.
Shuffle Curve Sprint
Pairs, backs to each other at one end of a curved cone setup. On go — side shuffle through the curve, race back around, sprint through to the start. First one through wins.
Linear Sprint Drop Drill 4x min
Two players race. Pick up a cone, sprint to set distance, put it down, sprint back. Repeat until all cones moved. Solo or team vs team.
Lateral Sprint Drop Drill 4x min
Same as linear but lateral only — side shuffling the whole time. Loads the hips and groin. If it feels easy it's not being done right.
Burpee to Sprint 4x min
Full burpee → sprint immediately. Trains acceleration out of fatigue. The burpee is the load — sprint starts the moment they're upright.
